The bank bonus is a $126.28 wage with a four-bank ceiling

The offer reads like free money: $300 for opening a checking account and running two $500 direct deposits through it inside 90 days. The deposits do not have to be a paycheck; an ACH push from a brokerage account carries the same payroll tag. The $1,000 routed through stays liquid the whole time, it just has to sit parked while the clock runs.

The work is short and spread out. Opening the account takes 30 minutes. Wiring the two deposit pushes takes 20 more. Tracking the 90-day window and the payout schedule runs about 35 minutes over a cycle, and closing the account before any monthly fee can attach takes 15. All in: 100 minutes, which is 1.6667 hours. The gross wage is therefore $300 / 1.6667 hours = $180 an hour, computed on active minutes only.

The IRS takes its cut as interest income. A bank bonus lands on a 1099-INT and is taxed like wages at the margin, so at a 24 percent bracket the keep is $300 x 0.76 = $228. That re-prices the hour at $228 / 1.6667 hours = $136.80, still more than five times the $25 a delivery shift clears after mileage.

Stack four banks across a year with openings spaced 90 days apart and the ChexSystems file stays clean, which is what keeps the rotation sustainable. Four cycles pay 4 x $228 = $912 and carry 4 x 100 minutes = 400 minutes of work. The parked money has a cost too: $1,000 held for the 160 days between the first deposit and the payout, 90 to meet the terms and about 70 for the bank to cut the check, forgoes $1,000 x 0.04 x 160/365 = $17.53 per cycle at current savings rates. Net of the four interest charges, 912 - 70.12 = $841.88, and the true wage lands at $841.88 / 6.6667 hours = $126.28 an hour.

The rate holds up; the ceiling is the problem. A gig scales with hours. Churning scales with banks, and banks cap new accounts by ChexSystems history, so the rotation tops out at four cycles a year no matter how fast the minutes go. $912 gross, $841.88 net, is the whole year, paid in four lumps with zero schedule risk. At $126.28 an hour the work pays 5.05 times the $25 delivery floor, and it cannot be bought in bulk, which is why it stays a supplement and never a replacement income.
